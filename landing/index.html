What it can and can’t see

Hosting, payments, auth, analytics and support tools are detected reliably — they have to run in your browser to work at all, so there is real evidence for them.

AI models usually cannot be seen. When a site asks GPT or Claude something, that conversation happens between its servers and the model provider; your browser is never part of it. Deep scan recovers what it can from the code the page already loaded, and StackLens stays quiet rather than guessing.

There is no confidence score in the panel, because a number like “91% confident” sounds precise while meaning very little. Instead, anything StackLens isn’t genuinely sure about simply isn’t shown.

Private by design

No account, no server, no analytics, no tracking. Nothing about the sites you visit is ever transmitted anywhere — everything is analysed on your own device and discarded when you close your browser.

Cookie and storage names are read to identify services; their values never are. Brand logos are bundled inside the extension rather than loaded from a CDN, specifically so no third party learns which sites you scan.
